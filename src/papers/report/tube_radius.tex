\section*{Source}

This note summarizes the double-integrator conformal tube radius used by
\path{fine_tuning/scripts/dconformal_contraction_verify.py}.  The numerical
example below uses the current report snapshot:
\[
\text{\path{fine_tuning/plots/dconformal_contraction_verify_1849.json}}.
\]

For that run:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{sample\_id} &= 1858, &
\text{calibration samples} &= 1850,\\
s_u &= 0.836826, &
s_x &= 0.268212,\\
q_u &= 0.651208, &
q_x &= 0.386208,\\
\delta_u &= 0.15, &
\delta_x &= 0.15.
\end{aligned}
\]
The reported joint probability lower bound is therefore
\[
1-\delta_u-\delta_x = 1-0.15-0.15 = 0.70.
\]

\section*{Tube Formula}

The code computes the radius at each time sample as
\[
r(t)
=
\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\min}}}
\|x(0)-x_d(0)\| e^{-\alpha t}
+
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}}{\alpha\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}
\left(\|B\| q_u + \|BK\| q_x\right)
\left(1-e^{-\alpha t}\right).
\]

The first term is the decaying initial tracking error.  The second term is the
conformal uncertainty contribution from control mismatch and state/reference
mismatch.

\section*{Fixed Values for the Current LQR Setup}

These values are fixed as long as the double-integrator model and LQR weights in
\path{fine_tuning/scripts/lqr.py} are unchanged:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\min}(P) &= 0.6126217787,\\
\lambda_{\max}(P) &= 9.8122021157,\\
\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}} &= 4.0020916201,\\
\alpha &= 1.5310952658,\\
\|B\|_2 &= 1.1,\\
\|BK\|_2 &= 3.9937691073.
\end{aligned}
\]

The common multiplier in the steady uncertainty term is
\[
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}}{\alpha\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}
= 2.6138749883.
\]

\section*{Numerical Calculation}

Using the current quantiles:
\[
q_u = 0.651208, \qquad q_x = 0.386208,
\]
the uncertainty contribution inside the parentheses is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|B\|q_u &= 1.1(0.651208) = 0.7163288,\\
\|BK\|q_x &= 3.9937691073(0.386208) = 1.5424256,\\
\|B\|q_u+\|BK\|q_x &= 2.2587544.
\end{aligned}
\]

The asymptotic, or steady-state, radius contribution is
\[
r_\infty
= 2.6138749883(2.2587544)
= 5.9041016\ \text{m}.
\]

For the plotted trajectory, the largest tube radius occurs at approximately
\[
t = 2.5\ \text{s}.
\]
At this time,
\[
e^{-\alpha t}
= e^{-1.5310952658(2.5)}
= 0.0217588.
\]

The initial tracking error for this run is zero:
\[
\|x(0)-x_d(0)\| = 0.
\]
Therefore the decaying initial-error term contributes zero, and the finite-time
radius is
\[
\begin{aligned}
r(2.5)
&= 0
+ 5.9041016(1-0.0217588)\\
&= 5.7756356\ \text{m}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This matches the reported maximum tube radius:
\[
\texttt{max\_tube\_radius\_m} = 5.775636.
\]

\section*{What Is Fixed vs. What Can Change}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.28\linewidth}
                >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.62\linewidth}}
\hline
Quantity & Status \\
\hline
$B$, damping, $Q$, $R$ & Fixed unless the LQR/model setup in \texttt{lqr.py} is changed.\\
$P$, $K$, $\alpha$ & Derived from solving the Riccati equation; fixed only while $A$, $B$, $Q$, and $R$ are fixed.\\
$\lambda_{\min}$, $\lambda_{\max}$ & Eigenvalues of $P$; change if $P$ changes.\\
$\|B\|$, $\|BK\|$ & Fixed for a fixed $B$ and $K$.\\
$q_u$, $q_x$ & Change with calibration CSV, sample count, $\delta_u$, $\delta_x$, scoring method, and trajectory scheduling.\\
$s_u$, $s_x$ & Per-sample scores; change from sample to sample.\\
$\|x(0)-x_d(0)\|$ & Initial tracking error; changes with initial condition.\\
$t$ and horizon & Change with trajectory duration and simulation timestep.\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section*{Why the Radius Can Be Larger Than Expected}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The actual tracking error is not the tube radius.  For this run,
\[
\max_t \|x(t)-x_d(t)\| = 0.306533\ \text{m},
\]
while the certified tube radius is
\[
5.775636\ \text{m}.
\]
The tube is a conservative bound, not the realized error.

\item The state quantile $q_x$ is not only position error.  In this code,
\[
x = [p_x,\ p_y,\ v_x,\ v_y],
\]
so velocity mismatch contributes to $q_x$.

\item The $q_x$ term is strongly amplified:
\[
\|BK\|q_x = 3.9937691073 q_x.
\]
Then the whole uncertainty term is multiplied again by
\[
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}}{\alpha\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}
= 2.6138749883.
\]

\item The conformal tube assumes the calibrated uncertainty can persist over
time.  That is why the uncertainty term grows with
\[
1-e^{-\alpha t}
\]
toward a steady radius.

\item Changing the calibration quantiles changes the tube directly.  Larger
$q_u$ or $q_x$ increases the radius linearly.
\end{enumerate}
